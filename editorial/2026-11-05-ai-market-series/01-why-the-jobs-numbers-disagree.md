# AI is spreading. Why do the jobs numbers disagree?

Status: unpublished research brief; editorial review pending. Research cutoff: November 5, 2026. Proposed format: 1,300–1,600-word market explainer. Audience: all Skynet Jobs readers, especially people trying to reconcile alarming headlines with hiring at their own company.

## The angle

Build the series around a useful distinction: a company, an occupation, and the whole economy can move in different directions at the same time. AI adoption is an input; employment, hiring, output, and wages are separate outcomes. The article should teach readers how to interpret a claim before deciding whether to believe it.

## Proposed opening

Your employer buys more AI software and keeps hiring. Your friend's team loses positions. A new graduate cannot get an interview. Which story describes the AI economy? Potentially all three. The question is whether the numbers being compared describe the same workers, employers, and period.

These are illustrative situations, not reported individuals.

## Evidence to build on

- [BEA's October spotlight](https://apps.bea.gov/scb/spotlights/2026/1026-ai-utilization.htm) finds stronger output trajectories and generally positive, less precisely estimated employment differences in higher-use state-industry cells. It explicitly leaves causality unresolved. **Classification: observational research.**
- [Ramp–Revelio's June paper](https://ramp.com/data/ai-jobs-impact/paper) studies 21,559 firms through February 2026. Its preferred comparisons associate high-intensity adoption with employment expansion; low-intensity estimates are not statistically significant. Adopters were already selected, faster-growing firms. **Classification: quasi-experimental estimate with identification limits.**
- [Federal Reserve hiring research](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/ai-adoption-and-firms-job-posting-behavior-20260327.html) finds no aggregate posting reduction in its comparisons through November 2025, while allowing for losses within particular occupations. **Classification: observational/proxy-based research.**
- [The Fed's adoption-methods note](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html) explains why survey units and wording produce different adoption estimates. **Classification: measurement analysis.**

## Structure

1. Open with the apparent contradiction, then identify each unit of analysis.
2. Draw the sequence from technical capability to deployment, changed tasks, staffing decisions, and labor-market outcomes. Explain why each step needs its own evidence.
3. Compare firm-level growth with occupation-level pressure. A growing employer can change whom it hires; economy-wide totals can conceal distributional costs.
4. Explain selection and the counterfactual in plain language: what would these firms have done without adoption?
5. Give readers five questions for the next AI employment headline: who, when, compared with whom, measured how, and how certain?

## Counterargument and limits

A muted aggregate effect does not mean disruption is harmless or will remain small. Conversely, a compelling layoff announcement cannot measure a national causal effect. Avoid declaring either a jobs apocalypse or definitive vindication of AI-led growth. Do not describe older observation windows as current-month statistics.

## Reader value and visual

Offer a one-page evidence-reading guide. An original comparison table should show **unit / adoption measure / outcome / window / causal limitation** for the three employment studies. Do not average their effect estimates or put unlike measures on one percentage-change chart.
