# AI Layoff Attribution

This note explains how Skynet Jobs classifies layoffs that may be connected to AI, automation, or AI-era restructuring.

## Core principle

Do not collapse every AI-linked layoff into a direct replacement story.

A company can truthfully say AI did not directly replace a specific worker and still be cutting roles partly because AI changed the productivity baseline, the capital plan, the org design, or the amount of labor leadership is willing to carry.

## Evidence labels

### Company stated AI

Use when the company, executive, SEC filing, official memo, or official blog directly connects the workforce change to AI, automation, agents, machine learning, or AI productivity.

Examples of supporting language:

- AI adoption resulted in workforce reductions.
- AI agents are handling work previously handled by staff.
- Automation or machine learning enabled reductions in a function.
- Headcount is being reduced while work is shifted into AI-enabled systems.

### AI-linked restructuring

Use when AI is materially linked to the restructuring, but direct replacement is not claimed.

Examples:

- AI-native org design.
- Reallocation toward AI infrastructure or AI products.
- Flattening management layers because AI changes coordination costs.
- Moving workers into AI-focused initiatives while reducing other roles.
- Cutting non-AI roles while hiring aggressively into AI, data center, security, or applied AI work.

### Analyst attribution

Use when credible reporting or analyst consensus links the layoff to AI, but the company did not clearly state it.

This label should be used sparingly and reviewed when better primary evidence appears.

## AI link score

The AI link score is a directional evidence-strength estimate.

It is not:

- a causal probability;
- a claim that AI directly replaced every affected worker;
- a claim that the company would not have cut jobs without AI;
- a worker-level displacement estimate.

Suggested bands:

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 90-100 | Direct company/filer AI connection or clear tool-driven displacement signal |
| 70-89 | Strong AI-linked restructuring or reallocation signal |
| 40-69 | Mixed signal, analyst attribution, or multiple non-AI business drivers |
| 0-39 | Too weak for the public AI-attributed tracker except as a watchlist or negative example |

## Review questions

Before adding a layoff event, ask:

1. Did the company directly cite AI, automation, agents, or machine learning?
2. Did the company cite AI-native restructuring, productivity expectations, or AI infrastructure reallocation?
3. Are the affected roles plausibly exposed to the cited AI workflow?
4. Are there stronger non-AI drivers such as merger integration, cyclical demand, gaming-specific cuts, or ordinary cost reduction?
5. Is the source primary, reputable reporting, or analyst interpretation?
6. Should the event be included, watched, or excluded as too weak?
