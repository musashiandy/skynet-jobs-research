# Sources for the November AI market article series

Accessed: **2026-11-05, America/New_York**. Related work: [ten article briefs](../editorial/2026-11-05-ai-market-series/README.md). Status: source research for unpublished editorial proposals; no scoring or tracker changes.

S01–S21 are local citation identifiers. Dates below are publisher dates unless marked otherwise. Opened sources were read for the claims used; abstract-only access is explicitly identified. A source's inclusion does not endorse all of its conclusions.

## S01 — AI Utilization and Changes in Economic Performance

- Publisher/author: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, *Survey of Current Business* staff, summarizing work by Christos A. Makridis, Tina Highfill, and Jon D. Samuels.
- Published: October 2, 2026. [Research spotlight](https://apps.bea.gov/scb/spotlights/2026/1026-ai-utilization.htm).
- Evidence: observational state-by-industry analysis; worker and employer adoption measures have different units and definitions. Supports examining expanding output alongside employment, rather than assuming displacement.
- Limits: reverse causality, thin cells, and mismatched measurement periods prevent a clean causal attribution. BEA publication is not an official estimate of jobs created by AI. The spotlight summarizes a working paper.
- Used in: 01.

November 7 clarification: the [working-paper record](https://www.bea.gov/research/papers/2026/ai-utilization-and-economic-performance) specifies 2025–2026 usage measures linked retrospectively to 2016–2024 outcomes. The full draft states those windows; see the [refresh supplement](2026-11-07-ai-market-series-draft-refresh.md).

## S02 — Monitoring AI Adoption in the U.S. Economy

- Author/publisher: Jeffrey S. Allen, Federal Reserve Board, *FEDS Notes*.
- Published: April 3, 2026. [Note](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html).
- Window: survey comparisons through 2025. Supports explaining firm counts, workers' own use, and employment-weighted employer adoption as different measures.
- Limits: sampling, question wording, and respondent knowledge differ; Census wording changed in late 2025. Historical adoption levels are not November 2026 estimates. Research views are the author's.
- Used in: 01, 03.

## S03 — A New Look at AI's Impact on Jobs: Firm-Level AI Spending and Workforce Adjustment

- Authors/publishers: Ara Kharazian and Ryan Stevens, Ramp; Lisa Simon, Revelio Labs.
- Published: June 30, 2026, per paper. [Landing page](https://ramp.com/data/ai-jobs-impact); [full paper](https://ramp.com/data/ai-jobs-impact/paper).
- Window: January 2021–February 2026; 21,559 U.S. firms. Links paid AI adoption to profile-derived workforce records; compares adopters with not-yet adopters within eventual spending-intensity groups.
- Limits: selected commercial sample, endogenous adoption, and some pre-trend flags. Table 3's headline is an average over post-adoption months 0–24, not the month-24 chart endpoint or raw national job growth. Not randomized evidence.
- Used in: 01; comparison lead for 02.

## S04 — Canaries Dashboard

- Publisher/research team: Stanford Digital Economy Lab with ADP Research; based on work by Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar, and Ruyu Chen.
- Published: living dashboard; no single publication date displayed. [Dashboard](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/project/indicators/canaries-dashboard/), accessed November 5, 2026. Page text references its July 2026 release.
- Evidence: employment indices by age, occupation, and modeled AI exposure in a balanced ADP client sample.
- Limits: payroll sample is not all employers; age is not tenure; exposure is not actual adoption. Chart data are revisable. No new numerical endpoint was extracted or asserted in this packet.
- Used in: 02.

## S05 — What is really happening to jobs? Separating AI hype from reality

- Authors/publisher: Neale Mahoney, Erika McEntarfer, and Karsen Wahal; Stanford Institute for Economic Policy Research.
- Published: July 2026; day not displayed. [Policy brief](https://siepr.stanford.edu/publications/policy-brief/what-really-happening-jobs-separating-ai-hype-reality).
- Evidence: research synthesis examining employment, recent graduates, productivity, and adoption; useful counterarguments about interest rates, pandemic hiring, remote work, and timing.
- Limits: synthesis, not a new comprehensive causal estimate. Underlying studies differ in coverage and dates; the brief does not settle AI attribution.
- Used in: 02; contextual reading for 01.

## S06 — AI and Job Postings: From Destruction to Creation?

- Author/publisher: Guillermo Gallacher, Indeed Hiring Lab.
- Published: July 8, 2026. [Analysis](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/).
- Window: charts through June 2026; seniority decomposition May 2025–May 2026. Original platform vacancy data support distinguishing recovery from pre-pandemic levels and examining which roles contribute to gains.
- Limits: vacancies are not hires; platform coverage and classification matter. Coincidence with agent launches does not identify their causal effect. Seniority and AI-title contributions can overlap.
- Used in: 02, 05, 10.

## S07 — We are Changing our Developer Productivity Experiment Design

- Authors/publisher: Joel Becker, Nate Rush, Tom Cunningham, David Rein, and Khalid Mahamud; METR.
- Published: February 24, 2026. [Research update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/).
- Window: initial February–June 2025 experiment and follow-up begun August 2025. Supports explaining how participant/task selection, compensation changes, and concurrent agents compromised follow-up interpretation.
- Limits: does not establish a reliable universal 2026 speedup. Earlier randomized findings retain their original setting and period; later uncertainty does not erase them.
- Used in: 05.

## S08 — Measuring the Self-Reported Impact of Early-2026 AI on Technical Worker Productivity

- Contributor/publisher: Joel Becker, METR.
- Published: May 11, 2026. [Study](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/).
- Window: February–April 2026 survey of 349 technical workers. Distinguishes perceived speed from perceived value of work.
- Limits: convenience sample, low response rates, retrospective judgments, and unvalidated counterfactuals. Self-reports are not measured output or national productivity statistics.
- Used in: 04, 05.

## S09 — Ambient AI Scribes in Clinical Practice: A Randomized Trial

- Authors/publisher: Paul J. Lukac and colleagues, *NEJM AI*; UCLA study.
- Published online: November 26, 2025; December 2025 issue. Public manuscript available January 6, 2026. [Indexed full manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12768499/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2501000).
- Window: November 4, 2024–January 3, 2025; 238 outpatient physicians, 14 specialties, two products and usual-care control.
- Limits: one institution, English encounters, evolving products, secondary outcomes, occasional reported inaccuracies. Does not test job elimination or establish autonomous documentation safety. Manuscript text was available in search retrieval; direct PMC opening intermittently returned a browser challenge.
- Used in: 06.

## S10 — Comparing ambient scribes: a randomized crossover clinical trial addressing ambient scribe technologies' impact on physician burnout

- Authors/publisher: Anand Chowdhury and colleagues, *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association*, Duke study.
- Published: May 1, 2026 issue, 33(5):990–999. [PubMed abstract and citation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41729180/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag018).
- Evidence: open-label crossover trial of 160 clinicians; 136 survey respondents; product comparisons of satisfaction, burnout, and documentation metrics.
- Limits: reviewed abstract, not embargoed full manuscript. Fieldwork dates not established from abstract. Does not estimate workforce reductions or compare every product with no AI.
- Used in: 06.

## S11 — The Ambulatory Patient Experience with Ambient Artificial Intelligence Scribes: A Qualitative Study

- Authors/publisher: Oliver T. Nguyen and colleagues, *Journal of General Internal Medicine*, University of Wisconsin/UW Health research.
- Published online: October 19, 2026. [PubMed abstract and citation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42897388/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-026-10931-4).
- Evidence: interviews with 20 patients about communication and comfort during scribe implementation.
- Limits: abstract reviewed; fieldwork dates not established. Small qualitative sample cannot establish population satisfaction, diagnostic accuracy, safety, or staffing effects. Disclosed conflicts warrant checking in final reporting.
- Used in: 06.

## S12 — Clio's 2026 Legal Trends Report reveals record gains in billable time as AI reshapes law firm economics

- Publisher: Clio, first-party research announcement.
- Published: October 26, 2026. [Release](https://www.clio.com/about/press/2026-legal-trends-report/).
- Evidence: surveys, anonymized operating data, and qualitative interviews. Supports examining the allocation of legal-service productivity gains and reported staffing patterns.
- Limits: legal AI vendor; adopter comparisons are not randomized. Its 42% billable-workday figure is a 2026 projection; staffing evidence includes respondent attribution. Detailed field dates and sampling require full-report review before publication.
- Used in: 07.

## S13 — Future of Professionals Report 2026: Actionable insights for law firm leaders

- Publisher: Thomson Reuters Institute.
- Published: 2026 edition; precise page publication date not displayed. [Legal report](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/future-of-professionals-2026/report-legal).
- Window: March–April 2026; 736 law-firm responses in 46 countries, plus references to 203 corporate legal responses.
- Limits: vendor-sponsored survey and strategic interpretation; client expectations are not verified delivery outcomes. Do not combine denominators with other editions or Clio's sample.
- Used in: 07.

## S14 — Barclays Q3 2026 Results Presentation

- Publisher: Barclays PLC.
- Published: October 22, 2026. [Official PDF, slide 10 / physical PDF page 10](https://home.barclays/content/dam/home-barclays/documents/investor-relations/ResultAnnouncements/Q32026Results/Q326-Results-Presentation.pdf#page=10).
- Evidence: company expectation of approximately 5,000 FTE reductions associated with FY26 structural cost actions, subject to consultation. AI, technology change, and organizational simplification appear together.
- Limits: expectation is not completed separations, task-by-task replacement, a uniquely AI-caused total, or net group employment change. Support-function proportion has an H2 structural-cost-action qualifier. Slide text and consultation footnote reviewed in the original PDF.
- Used in: 03.

## S15 — Five Million Robots now Operate in Factories Globally

- Publisher: International Federation of Robotics (IFR).
- Published: September 24, 2026. [World Robotics release](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally).
- Window: 2025 industrial robot stock and installations, with geography-specific differences.
- Limits: trade-association adoption statistics, not employment outcomes; robot stock and annual installations are different measures. Industrial automation need not use generative AI.
- Used in: 08.

## S16 — Global Sales of Professional Service Robots Surge 24%

- Publisher: IFR.
- Published: September 30, 2026. [World Robotics service-robot release](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-sales-of-professional-service-robots-surge-24-percent).
- Window: 2025 shipments/sales; distinguishes logistics applications and a separately described humanoid segment.
- Limits: category definitions, trade-association coverage, and different robot types matter. Counts do not establish productive operating hours, equivalent human labor, or layoffs. Humanoids can require teleoperation.
- Used in: 08.

## S17 — Upwork's Future Workforce Index 2026

- Publisher: Upwork Research Institute / Upwork Inc.
- Published: July 14, 2026. [Investor-relations release and methodology](https://investors.upwork.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upworks-future-workforce-index-2026-how-ai-redefining-value-work).
- Window: March–April 2026 survey of 2,400 selected U.S. skilled workers plus platform comparisons reported year over year; exact platform-period endpoints not stated in release.
- Limits: marketplace and selected survey populations differ; LLM classification of AI work; commercial interest. Per-contract earnings are neither hourly pay nor total worker income. Broader freelancer-share claims exclude many workers by design.
- Used in: 09.

## S18 — Payrolls to Prompts: Firm-Level Evidence on the Substitution of Labor for AI

**November 7 source alert:** full HTML review identified incorrect event-month wording in the identification discussion. This source is excluded from the completed article, which uses S24 instead. See the [refresh supplement](2026-11-07-ai-market-series-draft-refresh.md). The original notes below are retained as the November 5 record.

- Author/publisher: Ryan Stevens, arXiv working paper, version 1.
- Submitted: January 28, 2026. [Abstract and paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00139v1).
- Window: Q3 2021–Q3 2025. Abstract describes payments to online labor marketplaces and AI providers, using a difference-in-differences design around ChatGPT's release.
- Limits: preprint; abstract-level review only. Spending shifts do not identify individual freelancer job losses or equal-quality replacement; other changes can confound the common-shock design. This author also coauthors S03, so the two are not fully independent corroboration.
- Used in: 09.

## S19 — ActivTrak Productivity Lab: AI Users Have Workdays Up to 90 Minutes Longer Than Non-Users

- Publisher: ActivTrak; issuer-provided release distributed by PR Newswire.
- Published: October 29, 2026, 10:00 ET. [Release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activtrak-productivity-lab-ai-users-have-workdays-up-to-90-minutes-longer-than-non-users-302923731.html).
- Window: April 1–September 25, 2026; 9,140 knowledge workers at 467 enterprises; digital activity and workday-span comparisons.
- Limits: observational, selected monitored workplaces, commercial interest. Workday span is not continuous effort, output, or a causal AI effect. Full cohort adjustment and measurement methods are not provided in this release.
- Used in: 04.

## S20 — Workforce in motion: Skills and pathways to future jobs in the United States

- Authors/publisher: María Jesús Ramírez, Kweilin Ellingrud, Tanguy Catlin, Diego Castresana, and Anna Kortis; McKinsey Global Institute.
- Published: September 29, 2026. [Report and methodology access](https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/our-research/workforce-in-motion-skills-and-pathways-to-future-jobs-in-the-united-states).
- Horizon: U.S. labor-demand and transition scenarios over the next decade, including 2035 outcomes; skill, wage, credential, and location barriers.
- Limits: modeled pathways and demand scenarios involving AI, other automation, demographics, and broader growth. Not an observed layoff count, guaranteed vacancy forecast, or personalized retraining recommendation.
- Used in: 10.

## S21 — AI Adoption and Firms' Job-Posting Behavior

- Authors/publisher: Jessica Liu and Douglas Webber, Federal Reserve Board, *FEDS Notes*.
- Published: March 27, 2026. [Note](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/ai-adoption-and-firms-job-posting-behavior-20260327.html).
- Window: September 2023–November 2025. Lightcast postings linked to industry adoption and a separate firm-level hiring-based AI proxy; no detected aggregate posting reduction in the studied comparisons.
- Limits: postings are not hires or wage outcomes; proxies and aggregations can conceal occupational losses. Study deliberately stops at the Census question-wording break; not a November 2026 labor-market reading.
- Used in: 01.
