# Sources

This file tracks public source families used or planned for Skynet Jobs research. Entries should be expanded over time with specific documents, URLs, publication dates, access dates, and notes.

## Labor and employment data

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)

Used for labor-market context, occupational categories, employment patterns, and baseline workforce data.

Recommended source notes to add:

- Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS)
- Employment Projections
- Occupational Outlook Handbook
- Layoff and separation-related public datasets where applicable

Caveat: BLS data is authoritative for U.S. labor statistics, but it does not directly measure AI-attributable displacement for most occupations.

## Automation and job exposure research

### Frey and Osborne

Used historically as a reference point for occupation-level automation exposure research.

Caveat: early automation-risk studies should not be treated as direct forecasts of current generative AI displacement. Use as historical context, not final truth.

### McKinsey Global Institute

Used for scenario analysis around automation, labor demand, skills transitions, and workplace adoption.

Caveat: McKinsey outputs often present scenarios and estimates, not precise predictions.

### Goldman Sachs Research

Used for macro-level estimates around generative AI exposure, productivity, and potential labor-market impact.

Caveat: high-level exposure estimates should be interpreted separately from actual displacement.

## Robotics and physical automation

### International Federation of Robotics (IFR)

Used for industrial robot installation and adoption trends.

Caveat: robot installation counts are useful for automation adoption trends but do not map directly to job loss without sector and geography context.

## Workforce outlook and skills

### World Economic Forum (WEF)

Used for employer survey context, job transformation narratives, skills demand, and future-of-work framing.

Caveat: survey-based forecasts reflect employer expectations and should be labeled separately from observed outcomes.

## AI adoption and productivity studies

Source families to track:

- academic studies on generative AI productivity effects;
- field experiments in software, customer support, writing, legal, and knowledge work;
- industry reports on enterprise AI adoption;
- reputable survey data on AI tool usage.

Caveat: productivity gains vary heavily by task, worker skill level, workflow design, and measurement period.

## Source quality notes

Prefer primary sources, peer-reviewed research, official statistics, and original publisher data. Secondary analysis may be useful, but it should be labeled as interpretation.
